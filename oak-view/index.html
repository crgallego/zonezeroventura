The local snapshot

Where Oak View stands

Oak View lines Highway 33 between Ojai and the coast, the valley's corridor community, unincorporated, inside the Ojai Valley's confirmed Very High zone, and served by the Ventura County Fire Department. Zone Zero (Zone 0) applies to structures here, and the community's corridor geography, homes stepping up from the highway toward brushy slopes on both sides, makes the parcel-level five feet an especially concrete conversation.

The local record

The corridor's shared 2017 memory

Oak View shared the Ojai Valley's Thomas Fire experience in December 2017: the fire burned the surrounding terrain while Highway 33, the corridor's lifeline, carried the valley's evacuations. For a community strung along one road between slopes, the lesson was about both preparation and egress, and the preparation half is what each parcel controls. The ember-resistant ring at every structure, house, garage, and outbuilding alike, is the requirement; the slopes beyond five feet fall under the broader defensible-space zones the free assessment also covers.

Questions

Quick answers

Does Zone Zero apply in Oak View?

Yes. Oak View sits inside the Ojai Valley's confirmed Very High zone as an unincorporated community under the Ventura County Fire Department, so ember-resistant zone requirements apply to structures here.

What makes Oak View's situation specific?

Corridor geography: homes stepping from Highway 33 toward brushy slopes on both sides, with one main route through. The parcel-level five feet is the controllable half of that equation, and it starts with the fence line and the mulch bed.

Do outbuildings count under Zone 0?

Yes. The ember-resistant zone wraps every structure: house, garage, sheds, and outbuildings each get the first-five-feet treatment.
